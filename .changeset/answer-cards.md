Open the HTML report on the answer. `codeheat analyze --html` now starts with the question "does your design hold up to the way your code actually changes?", with the verdict beside it and four answer cards below, and keeps the treemap, its side panel, color modes, and filter in a "Map" section under them, one link away in the bar.

- The verdict is a badge for the whole repository: holds up, holds in parts, or under strain, by the share of the change effort in territories that leak (at most 75% of their changes stay inside and another territory shares changes with them, the gate of a `boundary` place to start; a territory with fewer than `minModuleCommits` changes, or with no partner to leak to, is not judged), one level worse when the design erodes. Beside it stands the trend of the share of changes that stay in one module, called a trend only from enough quarters on. With less than half of the change effort in territories that can be judged it says "Not enough evidence" and why. A window without counted changes says so, with when the history last changed: the day of the newest commit of the repository (`window.lastCommitAt`) and a longer `--since` when that commit lies before the window, that the window's commits are mechanical, or else the span of the latest quarter of the series with counted changes.
- Four cards each answer one part of the question with a number: where change concentrates (the share of all the change effort, test code included, in the three hottest territories), what changes together (the shared changes of the strongest pair of territories, drawn as a link, and the file pairs between territories with no import), where the structure is weak (how many of the territories with enough changes leak, and the effort they hold, every territory as a dot against the 75% line), and where to start (the share of the production code's heat in the territories of the top three places to start, and the top four with their design move and the code's heat each puts at stake).
- A card opens one compact chart below the cards: heat by territory (a territory there shows its files in the map), the pairs of territories that change together, how much of each territory's change stays inside and where it reaches most, or the places to start with their verdict, design move, and key numbers. A card with nothing to show (no territories, no counted changes, not enough to judge) says why in place of its number.
- Numbers are printed, never only colored, text colors meet WCAG AA, the page follows light and dark mode, and the cards stack on a phone.

The help of `--html`, `--out`, and `--no-open` now says "HTML report" instead of "treemap". The JSON report and the flags are unchanged.
