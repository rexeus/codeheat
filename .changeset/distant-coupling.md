Rank the places where structurally distant code keeps changing together, and add two scaling signals. `analyze --json` gains:

- `distantCouplings`: coupled pairs in different modules, or far apart within one module, without test code and pairs of two contract files. The score is `strength × reach × hidden × evidence`: `reach` is `1 + log2(1 + hops)` between the module directories (so deeply nested paths do not dominate) or the share of the largest file distance within one module, `hidden` is 1.5 for a pair no import links, and `evidence` is `min(1, sharedCommits / 10)`, so a pair that met in three commits does not outrank one that met in eleven.
- `moduleCoupling`: per pair of modules the shared counted commits and the share of the smaller module's commits, the data of a coupling matrix.
- `cliques`: groups of at least three modules of which every pair shares 30% of the smaller module's commits and that at least three commits touched in full, each with a one-line reason. A group in which no three commits touched every member is searched for its sub-groups that did; a clique inside another is dropped, and two variants of one unit (their union pairwise linked, nearly all members in common) are reported once, as the stronger. `cliquesPartial` is true when the bounded sub-group search gave up on a very varied group.
- `unstableInterfaces`: TypeScript and JavaScript files that many files import directly and that change more often than their dependents, with the dependents that changed with them.
- `dependencyDirection`: imports from a module that rarely changes to one that changes often (the Stable Dependencies Principle, measured from change rates). Each edge carries `ratio` (the imported module's commits over the importing one's) and `changesTogether` (counted commits in which a file of the importing module changed together with a file it imports); the list ranks by `log2(ratio) × log2(1 + changesTogether)`, so a framework that everything imports does not fill it.

`inspect` partners carry `distant`, `thresholds` report the new limits (`minLocalDistance`, `minCliqueShare`, `minFanIn`, `minInterfaceChanges`, `minVolatilityRatio`), and `--limit` cuts the new lists like the others. The terminal shows the five best distant couplings and a line per clique ahead of the plain coupling table, marks distant partners in `inspect`, and lists the first unstable interfaces and stable-to-volatile imports. Dependencies for the two scaling signals are read from every non-test code file, by the parser for the files of coupled pairs and by a text scan for the rest, which adds a few percent to the time of an analysis.
